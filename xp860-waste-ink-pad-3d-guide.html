Epson XP-860 · Waste ink pad tray (1611102)

This is the picture version of the guide. If you see this instead of a 3D model you can rotate, the app you opened it with does not run the interactive part. Open the file in Chrome or Safari for the 3D version.

Before you start

The waste ink tray (Epson part 1611102, “Tray, porous pad, ink eject”) sits in the bottom front-right corner of the printer, under the ink system. Reaching it means removing 3 plastic covers and 8 screws.

Tools: Phillips screwdriver (magnetic and slim-shafted if possible), small flat screwdriver, tweezers, gloves, paper towels, a plastic bag for the old tray.

Read this first. These steps come from a filmed replacement on the XP-750 and matching teardown photos of the same chassis family. I could not confirm them on an XP-860 itself, so treat screw positions as a close guide, not a guarantee. Details on the last page.

1 · Power off and unplug

Turn the printer off with its power button and wait for it to shut down fully, so the print head parks on the right. Then unplug the power cord.

Filmed XP-750 repair

2 · Lift the scanner unit

Lift the whole scanner unit, the same way as for changing ink cartridges (not just the document lid). You should be looking down at the cartridges.

Filmed XP-750 repair

3 · Remove the trim strip (2 black screws)

Along the front edge of the opening is a long black plastic strip A (just in front of the cartridge-replacement sticker).

  • Remove the 2 black screws: one near the left end, one toward the right.
  • Lift the strip up and out.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

4 · Remove the upper right cover (2 silver screws)

Removing the strip exposes 2 silver screws at the right-hand end.

  • Remove both. The right one sits deep in a well: use a magnetic driver, or lift it out with tweezers.
  • Lift the small tab with the flat screwdriver, then lift cover B up and pull it off.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

5 · Pull out the paper cassette

Slide the paper cassette completely out of the front and set it aside. This uncovers a screw low on the front-right corner and makes the printer easier to tilt later.

Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

6 · Remove the lower right cover (2 screws)

The lower front-right corner cover C is held by 2 screws:

  • Black screw, low on the front at the cover’s inner (left) edge, visible now the cassette is out.
  • Silver screw at the top right of the cover, exposed when cover B came off.

Take cover C off. You can now see the white ink system and the black tray below it.

Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

7 · Remove the tray’s front screw

One silver screw at the front holds the tray D to the frame. Remove it.

Do not touch or pull on the white ink system above the tray.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

8 · Tilt the printer, remove the bottom screw

The last screw is underneath, below the tray. It is shorter than the others, so keep it separate.

  • Either tip the printer onto its side, or slide it so the front-right corner hangs over the table edge (the feet stay on the table).
  • Remove the short screw.
A full tray can drip when tilted. Tilt gently, towels underneath. Hanging the corner over the table edge is the cleaner option.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

9 · Release the tabs, pull the tray down and out

The tray is still clipped in by plastic tabs.

  • Lift each tab slightly with the flat screwdriver (the video releases two spots) and pull the tray downward, out of the bottom of the printer.
  • Keep it level and put it straight in the bag. It is full of ink.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

10 · Fit the new tray

Compare the new tray with the old one: same shape, pads seated, opening on top.

  • Push it up into the same position, the same way round, so the printer’s ink outlet sits over the tray’s opening.
  • Press until the tabs click.
Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

11 · Refit the 2 tray screws

Refit the short screw underneath and the silver screw at the front. Snug only; they thread into plastic.

Then set the printer back down flat.

Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

12 · Refit covers, strip and cassette

Reverse of removal:

  • C lower cover: silver screw top right, black screw low on the front.
  • B upper cover: hook the lower edge in first, press until it seats, then its 2 silver screws (deep one on the right).
  • A trim strip: press down, 2 black screws.
  • Slide the paper cassette back in, lower the scanner unit.

All 8 screws should be back in. None left over.

Filmed XP-750 repairTeardown photos, same chassis

13 · Reset the waste ink counter

The error will not clear by itself. The printer counts ink, it does not sense the pad, so the counter has to be reset from a Windows PC over USB.

  • With the Epson Adjustment Program for this model: Waste ink pad counter → Main pad counter → Initialize, power the printer off and on, then Check should read 0%.
  • Or a paid reset key (WIC Reset utility), which pad sellers commonly bundle.
Only reset after the new tray is in. Resetting with a full pad lets ink overflow out of the printer.

How sure is this?

Confirmed
• Part: Epson’s XP-850 parts list shows item 507 = 1611102 “Tray, porous pad, ink eject assy”. The XP-860 is the same generation and is covered by the same Epson service manual (XP-850/860/800/810/820/830/750/760). Sellers of 1611102 list the XP-860.
• Location, step order and the 8 screws: from a filmed XP-750 replacement, independently matched by teardown photos of the same Expression Premium chassis.
Not confirmed
• I found no XP-860-specific teardown and could not open Epson’s service manual pages. The XP-860 adds a document feeder on top; I expect the front-right corner to match the XP-750, but have not seen it.
• Which way to tilt is your choice; the model shows one way.
• This 3D model is a diagram. Outer size is real (390 × 339 × 191 mm); cover shapes, screw sizes and exact positions are simplified.

Suggestion: check against the first real unit before sending this to customers as fact. If a screw is not where shown, look within a few centimetres; do not force any cover.
